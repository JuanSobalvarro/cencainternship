Celestial sphere is an arbitrary large abstraction of what we see in the universe from earth

Equatorial system

Based on the equator and the ecliptic intersection (vernal equinox) we use 2 angles as coordinates on the celestial sphere, right ascension and declination

Photometric concepts and magnitudes

- Luminosity: Total amount of energy an object emits per second in all directions (Watts W) aka total flux

- Flux ($F$): Flux is the amount of energy passing through a specific unit of area per second (W/m2)

- Flux Density ($F_\nu$ or $F_\lambda$): Since stars emit energy across the electromagnetic spectrum we should take in count the wavelength of the flux (W/m2/Hz aka Jansky Jy)

- Intensity ($I$): This is the flux received per unit of solid angle(steradian), intensity is independent of distance since it cancels out the flux drop with the greater area for the angle for a far distance

Time Variability

- Intrinsic variability. Source is changing in time: Pulsations, eruptions and flares, supernovae 

- Extrinsic. Signal is block by a external of the source phenomena: Stars orbiting each other, exoplanet orbiting, etc
